About this site

Challenging Beliefs examines the claims of other faiths against the Bible. It exists for one reason: not to win arguments, but to help real people meet the real Jesus — and to help Christians do that with both honesty and gentleness (1 Peter 3:15).

The rules every page follows

  • Steelman first. Every claim carries the strongest published defense from the faith's own scholars — FAIR and the Gospel Topics essays for Mormonism; IslamQA, Yaqeen Institute, and classical tafsir for Islam — before any verdict is rendered.
  • Cite their sources. Wherever possible, facts are documented from the faith's own canon and publications. A verdict of Admitted means their own sources concede the facts.
  • Name weak arguments as weak. Criticisms that don't survive scrutiny are marked Answered — including popular Christian polemics (the "moon god" myth, spread-only-by-the-sword). An examination that inflates weak claims forfeits the right to be believed on strong ones.
  • Verdicts are editorial. They describe the state of the public argument as best we can determine it — they are judgments, not revelations, and they cite their reasons.

Corrections & rebuttals — genuinely invited

If you are a Latter-day Saint or a Muslim and believe any claim here misstates the facts, misquotes a source, or omits your best response — we want to know. If a case can't survive its best counterargument, it doesn't belong here. Open an issue or write via the GitHub repository.

What this site is not

It is not a claim that Latter-day Saints or Muslims are foolish or insincere — most are neither, and many live lives of devotion that put Christians to shame. The dispute is about what is true, weighed from the documents. And it is not neutral: we believe the Bible stands, that Jesus is who the manuscripts say he claimed to be, and that "by grace are ye saved through faith… not of works" is the best news any person will ever hear.

Texts, translations & copyright

Bible quotations and the embedded Bible reader use the King James Version (public domain). Book of Mormon, Doctrine and Covenants, and Pearl of Great Price texts are drawn from public-domain early editions (the current LDS editions' headings and footnotes are not used). The Arabic Quran is public domain; the embedded English Quran reader uses the Pickthall translation (1930, public domain in the United States). Brief quotations from other translations (identified where quoted) and from church, apologetic, and scholarly publications appear as short attributed excerpts for purposes of criticism, commentary, and education under fair use (17 U.S.C. §107). Hadith excerpts are brief quotations with links to the full text on sunnah.com.
