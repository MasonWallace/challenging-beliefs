About this site

Which Gospel? examines the claims of other faiths against the Bible, and makes the case for the Bible itself where the reader does not already accept it. It exists for one reason: not to win arguments, but to help real people meet the real Jesus — and to help Christians do that with both honesty and gentleness (1 Peter 3:15).

The rules every page follows

  • Give the other side its best shot first. Every claim here is shown with the strongest published defence from that faith's own scholars before any verdict is given — a habit usually called steelmanning, the opposite of knocking down a straw man. FAIR and the Gospel Topics essays for Mormonism. IslamQA and the classical tafsir — the traditional Muslim verse-by-verse commentaries on the Quran — for Islam. The Watchtower's own publications for Jehovah's Witnesses. The camps' own teaching for Hebrew Israelites. Outreach Judaism and Jews for Judaism for the Messiah section. Graham Oppy and Bart Ehrman for the Case for God.
  • Cite their sources. Wherever possible, facts are documented from the faith's own canon and publications. A verdict of Admitted means their own sources concede the facts.
  • Name weak arguments as weak. Criticisms that don't survive scrutiny are marked so, including popular Christian arguments. Around forty entries on this site exist for one purpose: to tell Christians to stop using a particular argument — the "moon god" myth, "all twelve apostles were martyred", Darwin's deathbed, the Talmud quote lists, and more. An examination that inflates weak claims forfeits the right to be believed on strong ones.
  • The verdicts are our judgement, not God's. Each one describes where the public argument currently stands, as best we can tell, and shows the reasons behind it. They are calls we have made, and you can check our working.

The four grades, in plain words

Every case carries one of four grades. They are the most useful thing on the site, so here is what each one actually means for you.

  • Admitted. The other side's own books, essays or official statements concede the facts. You can make the point entirely from their sources, and there is nothing to argue about except what the facts mean. This is the strongest ground you can stand on.
  • Unrefuted. The claim has been put for a long time and no good answer has been published. Not "unanswerable" — just unanswered. Use it, and use it without gloating.
  • Contested. Scholars argue this one both ways and both sides have a real case. Say so before your friend does. Expect a conversation, not a win.
  • Don't use. The other side answers this one well, or the Christian version of the argument does not hold up. It is on the site so nothing is cherry-picked — and so you do not walk into it. Leading with these costs you credibility on everything else.

One section reads slightly differently: in the Hebrew Israelites section, a case marked Settled is one the biblical text answers clearly — the grievance behind it is still real, and the page says so.

Written to be read

Every case opens in plain English first — short blocks, one idea per sentence, aimed at about a ninth-grade reading level. The sourced version, with the scholars named and the citations in full, sits underneath it on the same page. Nothing is hidden and nothing is dumbed down; the same facts are simply said twice, once quickly and once completely.

Corrections & rebuttals — genuinely invited

If you are a Latter-day Saint, a Muslim, a Jehovah's Witness, a Hebrew Israelite, a Jewish reader or an atheist, and you believe any claim here misstates the facts, misquotes a source, or leaves out your best response — we want to know. If a case can't survive its best counterargument, it doesn't belong here. Open an issue or write via the GitHub repository.

What this site is not

It is not a claim that anyone examined here is foolish or insincere. Most are neither, and many live lives of devotion that put Christians to shame. The dispute is about what is true, weighed from the documents. And it is not neutral: we believe the Bible stands, that Jesus is who the manuscripts say he claimed to be, and that "by grace are ye saved through faith… not of works" is the best news any person will ever hear.
