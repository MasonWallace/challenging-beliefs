The same pattern, again and again

Mormonism, Islam, the Jehovah’s Witnesses and the Hebrew Israelites began centuries apart, on different continents. None of them copied the others. Yet all four ended up built on the same six structures. Read down a column to follow one movement. Read across a row to see why this page exists.

Why this page exists

Most people meet these movements one at a time, and each gets argued on its own terms: was Jerusalem destroyed in 607 or 587, does almah mean virgin, who were the Edomites. Those arguments matter, and the rest of this site is full of them.

Something else is visible only when you stand back. A movement that claims a new message from God correcting the Bible tends to need the same set of parts to work — someone who heard it privately, a reason the existing Scriptures can't be trusted, and a price for walking away. Those parts show up independently, again and again, in groups that would each be horrified to be compared with the others.

That recurrence is the argument — not "look how strange they are." A Muslim can point at the Book of Mormon and a Latter-day Saint can point at the Quran, and both would be doing the same useless thing. The point is that Scripture supplies a test that doesn't care which movement is in front of you, and that applies to us in exactly the same way.

The test that applies to all of them — and to us

Galatians 1:8

"But though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you than that which we have preached unto you, let him be accursed."Paul, to the churches of Galatia — decades before any movement in that table existed

Read what that verse actually does. It does not say an angel could not appear. It grants the possibility and removes its authority: the message is tested by the gospel already delivered, not the other way round. And Paul includes himself and his own team — though we — which is what makes it a test rather than a weapon.

That is why this is the right frame, and "look how strange the other one is" is not. If the Gabriel account cannot establish a gospel that corrects Paul's, then neither can the Moroni account, and neither can anything an angel says to me tomorrow. One rule, applied evenly, with no exemption written in for the person holding it.

Two further tests do the same work. Deuteronomy 18:22 — the prophet whose word does not come to pass has not spoken from the LORD, which is a public, checkable standard rather than a matter of interpretation. And 2 Corinthians 11:14 — Satan himself is transformed into an angel of light, which is precisely why a shining messenger cannot vouch for himself.

How to use this — and how not to

Apply the test to yourself first, out loud

"If an angel appeared to me tomorrow with a new gospel, I'd have to reject it too — Paul says so, and he includes himself." Say that before you apply it to anyone else. It turns an accusation into a shared standard, and it is the only honest version of this argument.

Use it to explain, not to corner

The most useful thing this page does in a real conversation is answer "why don't you accept our founder's revelation?" The answer isn't "because it's yours" — it's a rule that existed centuries before him and that rules out a great many claims your friend also rejects.

Be honest about where the parallel breaks

Three cells in that table say "not this pattern," because it's true. A parallel that has to be forced is worth less than none, and the moment you stretch one, everything else you've said becomes suspect.

Don't argue "your religion is as crazy as theirs"

This is the trap. It is the "well, you too" move — arguing by mutual embarrassment — and it proves nothing about whether either claim is actually true. It insults two people at once, and your friend will rightly notice the same move works against you.

Don't bring another religion into the room uninvited

Telling a Latter-day Saint about Muhammad usually produces a defense of Joseph Smith rather than an examination of him. This works best after someone has begun asking their own questions — as a frame for what they're already seeing, not as an opening move.

Don't let the pattern replace the evidence

Structural similarity is suggestive, not decisive. Two things sharing a shape can still differ in truth. The table is a lens for reading the cases; it is not a substitute for them, and a conversation that never reaches a specific document hasn't really happened.

The honest version of the argument

Stated fairly, it comes to this. Christianity makes a claim of exactly the kind that ought to be tested this way, and it invites the test: a public execution under a named Roman official, and a resurrection proclaimed within weeks in the very city where the body could have been produced, to hundreds of people who were still alive and could be asked. Paul stakes the entire religion on it and says plainly that if it did not happen, the whole thing is worthless.

That is a different shape of claim from a private revelation to one man that cannot, even in principle, be checked by anyone else. It is not that Christians are exempt from the test — it is that the test is the reason to look at the evidence, and the evidence is public. The Case for God lays that out from sources that do not want the conclusion.
