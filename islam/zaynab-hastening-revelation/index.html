He married his adopted son's ex-wife, Zaynab

Admitted

The facts sit in Islam's own trusted sources. You can make this point from their books alone.

What happened

Zaynab bint Jahsh was married to Zayd, Muhammad's adopted son. After they divorced, Quran 33:37 sanctioned Muhammad's marriage to her.

Under Arab custom that was a scandal. The verse says he concealed his interest and feared people's talk, and that Allah married her to him so there would be no blame on believers about the wives of adopted sons.

Quran 33:4-5 abolished adoption's legal force at the same time.

The privileges around it

Critics point to a pattern of verses. Quran 33:50 allows him unlimited wives, alone.

Quran 33:51 lets him reschedule his wives' turns. Quran 33:53 bans other men from marrying his widows.

What Aisha said

The sharpest line is inside the household. After 33:51 came down, Aisha said to him: "I feel that your Lord hastens in fulfilling your wishes and desires" (Sahih al-Bukhari 4788).

That is preserved in Islam's most authentic collection.

The Muslim answer, at full strength

ICRAA, Ibn Kathir and Maududi answer that this was costly reform, not indulgence. Arab adoption created false bloodlines with real inheritance and marriage effects.

Ending it needed a public case nobody could ignore, so God chose the hardest one. Muhammad resisted — you feared the people — which argues against opportunism.

Zaynab was his cousin, and he could have married her years earlier. Aisha's remark was spousal candour, and Muslims preserved it, which shows the tradition's honesty.

His marriages overall were mostly to widows and for alliances.

How strong is this argument?

They admit every fact, which is why this one lands. The sequence is in the Quran's own wording.

The privileges are explicit. The remark is Aisha's, in Bukhari.

The reform answer works for Zaynab alone but not for the pattern. Whether the pattern proves anything is a judgment.

That it exists is not in dispute.

What to say

"Aisha said that line herself, and your scholars kept it. What do you think she meant?"

How they answer this — at its strongest

They say the marriage was costly reform, and Muhammad resisted it.

ICRAA, Ibn Kathir and Maududi answer that the marriage was a costly legal reform, not indulgence. Adoption before Islam created false bloodlines, with real effects on inheritance and marriage. Ending it needed a public case nobody could ignore, and God chose the hardest one, the Prophet himself. No one else's example would have broken the taboo.

Muhammad resisted — you feared the people — which argues against opportunism. Zaynab was his cousin, and he could have married her years earlier had desire driven him. Aisha's remark was spousal candour. That Muslims faithfully kept even this barb in their most authentic collection shows the tradition's honesty. His marriages overall leaned toward widows and toward alliances.

The verdict

Strong for you: it is all in their books, down to Aisha's line.

Every factual element is canonical. The desire-then-revelation sequence is in the Quran's own wording. The privileges of 33:50-53 are explicit. And the line about a Lord who hastens to fulfil your desires is Aisha's, in Bukhari.

The legal-reform steelman holds together for Zaynab taken alone. It cannot explain the pattern as a whole. The argument that a preserved barb proves honesty also cuts both ways. It proves the tradition is honest, and that what it honestly preserves is suspicion inside the Prophet's own house at the time. Whether the pattern proves fabrication is a judgment call. That the pattern exists is admitted in Islam's canon.
