Don't use: '72 virgins' (the Quran gives no number)

Don't use

The Muslim answer holds up. Do not lead with this one.

The claim

That the Quran promises martyrs 72 virgins in paradise.

Why it is wrong

The number 72 appears nowhere in the Quran. The Quran promises houris — created, wide-eyed, virginal companions (Quran 56:22-24; 44:54; 55:56; 78:33) — with no count attached.

Where the number is

In a hadith. Jami at-Tirmidhi 1663 lists the martyr's six rewards, including marriage to "seventy-two wives from al-hur al-'ayn." The word is wives, not virgins.

It is graded sahih or hasan in the mainstream collections. But it is a hadith claim, not a Quranic one.

Why it backfires

Say the Quran promises 72 virgins and your friend truthfully replies that it does not. You lose on a citation error, and the real point escapes with it.

What to say instead

Be exact and the substance survives. The Quran's paradise is openly physical and sexual — that is this site's houris case.

And the 72-wives detail for martyrs is in a graded hadith. Cite it as Tirmidhi 1663 and the technicality defence evaporates.

What to say

"You are right that it is not in the Quran. It is Tirmidhi 1663.
