Colour schemes

Before proposing anything I measured what's on the site now. The palettes are mostly sound — ink, body text and every accent clear 5:1 comfortably. But three things are genuinely worth fixing, and one of them is an accessibility bug rather than a matter of taste.

What's actually wrong today

IssueMeasuredWhy it matters
--dim fails contrast in all three current palettes 2.37:1 light · 2.89:1 hub dark · 3.25:1 home It's real text — captions, eyebrows, keyboard hints, figure credits. Used 23 times on a single hub page. 4.5:1 is the readable minimum; light mode is at half that.
Cards barely separate from the page 1.08–1.10:1 panel vs background Every card depends entirely on its 1px border to exist. It's why the dark theme reads flat.
The accent identity is split, and the variable name lies --gold = #82b1d8 (blue) on hubs, #cf9a45 (amber) on the homepage The homepage says amber, every hub and article says blue. On hubs the real gold is called --amber. This already caused a bug when wiring the logo.

The constraint that shapes everything below: amber is already spoken for — --amber is the "Contested" verdict badge. So the logo's gold cannot become the site's UI accent without colliding with a verdict. In all three proposals the gold stays a brand colour used on the mark, and links and UI keep a distinct hue.

Three directions

Each keeps your four verdict hues distinguishable, and every single token below is verified at 4.5:1 or better against both its background and its card — including --dim.

My read

C (Deep Slate) is what I'd do. It is your current site, corrected — same cool, serious character, but cards actually sit above the page, --dim becomes readable, and blue is settled as the one UI accent so the gold belongs to the logo alone. Lowest risk, fixes all three problems.

B (Warm Paper) is the interesting one. A site of long-form reading about old manuscripts has a good reason to look like paper rather than a dashboard, and it would make you look nothing like every other apologetics site. It is the bigger change, and the warm dark mode is genuinely unusual — worth seeing before committing.

D (High Contrast) is the accessibility-first option: near-black on near-white, colour used only where it carries meaning. Cleanest to read, least personality.

Whichever you pick, the --gold/--amber naming should be straightened out at the same time — rename the blue to --acc everywhere so the token means what it says. That's a mechanical rename across the built pages and the two generators.
