The four things that actually moved people
1

No LDS lens, no manual. The single most common catalyst in every published testimony.

2

Answer worthiness exhaustion with grace

Forgiveness not conditional on performance is news to someone on the recommend treadmill.

3

Facts break faith in the system; a Christian who already loved them decides where they land — Christ or nothing.

4

Love without an agenda

Their culture knows “friendshipping.” A friendship that survives their leaving is the argument.

The rules

Witness Christ

Emphasize Jesus as substitute, not just example. See them as burdened, not as enemies.

Ask, don't assert

“What do you mean by that?” forces definitions; “How did you conclude that?” shifts the burden.

Cite only their sources

Gospel Topics essays, Joseph Smith Papers, church manuals. One overstated attack discredits ten true ones.

Never mock the sacred

Every yelled slogan functions as a testimony meeting for the Mormon hearing it.

Don't debate to win the visit

Plant one seed per conversation. Exits take years.

Pray

“No one can come to me unless the Father who sent me draws him” (John 6:44).

Sharing the gospel with Mormon friends

“I read the New Testament like a child, the way he asked me to. Eighteen months later I knew it would cost me everything — and I surrendered to Christ anyway.”

— a returned missionary, sent to convert a Baptist pastor who fed him instead

1 · It starts with a table, not an argument

The pastor didn't debate him. He fed him, learned his name, and made one request: read the Bible as a child. Love without an agenda is startling to someone from a conditional community — their culture has a word for friendship with a conversion timeline (“friendshipping”), and they can smell it.

2 · Say the sentence before the crisis

“If Mormonism turned out not to be true, that wouldn't mean Jesus isn't.”Said in advance, this routes a future faith crisis toward Christ instead of atheism — because the default landing after an “all true or all false” collapse is nothing.

3 · Ask questions from their own shelf

Cite only what they trust: the Gospel Topics essays, the Joseph Smith Papers, their own canon. “What do you mean by that?” does more than any lecture — and their book outranks yours in their hands, so argue from Moroni, not Isaiah.

4 · Answer exhaustion with grace

“I was so tired of never being enough. Someone told me Jesus said ‘It is finished’ — and I cried in my car.”The recommend-interview treadmill produces documented spiritual fatigue. Grace is the news.

5 · Be there when the shelf cracks — and stay

Facts break the shelf; a specific Christian who loved them without conditions decides where they land. Exits take years. If your friendship is conditional on agreement, you're offering the same deal the church does.

Memorize this

Plant one seed per conversation. Keep the friendship no matter what. Pray — “No one can come to me unless the Father draws him.”
