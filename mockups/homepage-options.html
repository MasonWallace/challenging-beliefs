Which Gospel?

Three layouts that fix the clipped wordmark, retire the stranded Hebrew Israelites card, and put all six sections plus the pattern piece and the about link on one screen.

What's wrong right now

I rendered your live homepage at three window sizes before touching anything. All three problems reproduce at every size.

  1. 1 The hero card is slicing through your wordmark.The g descenders in “Challenging” and “Beliefs” get cut off by the gold panel's top edge. It's caused by line-height:1.08 on the h1 — a serif face with deep descenders needs more room than that, and the header's bottom padding is measured in vh, so it shrinks further on short windows.
  2. 2 Hebrew Israelites is stranded.Four cards in a three-column grid leaves the fourth alone on its own row with two empty columns beside it — a wide gap of nothing in the middle of the page.
  3. 3 The six sections are split too far apart.“Making the case” sits far below “Examining other faiths,” so The Messiah Case and The Case for God are well past the fold. Most visitors never learn they exist.

For reference: what it looks like today

The clipping is subtle in a screenshot but obvious once you know to look — watch the bottom of both g's meet the gold border.

  • Fastest to scan if you already know where you're going
  • Room for the tagline under the wordmark

What it costs

  • Loses every blurb — a first-time visitor gets no idea what's inside each section

My read

Option B, because it's the only one that actually delivers what you asked for. I built these, measured them, and then had to correct myself: I'd assumed Option A would fit everything on one screen, and it doesn't quite — the bottom row's blurbs and the About bar land just under the fold on a 13-inch laptop. Option B clears it with room to spare.

The real trade is blurb length. Option A keeps your full three-line descriptions, which genuinely help someone arriving from a Google search for “horses in the Book of Mormon” know they're in the right place. Option B cuts each to a single line to buy the space. If those descriptions matter more to you than fitting on one screen, take A instead — it still fixes the clipped wordmark and the stranded card, which were the actual bugs. Option C is the fastest to scan and the least welcoming; it suits a reference tool more than a site meant to persuade.

Whichever you pick, the wordmark fix goes in either way: the heading's line height goes from 1.08 to 1.24 and the header's bottom padding stops being measured in vh, so the descenders can never be clipped again at any window size.
