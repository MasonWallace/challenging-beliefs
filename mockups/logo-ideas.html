Which Gospel? — logo directions

Six marks, each built around a book. All are pure line-art SVG in your existing palette (#cf9a45 gold on ink), so they scale from a 16px favicon to a hero header with no raster assets.

Each is shown large on dark, on light, and reversed on gold — plus a size ramp down to favicon scale, which is where weak logos fall apart.

01The Fork in the Road most literal to the name

An open book whose gutter rises and splits in two — one road grey, one gold. It says the exact thing the site's name asks: two gospels are on offer, only one of them goes anywhere. Reads instantly, no religious symbol required, and works for a non-Christian arriving from a search result.

02The Berean Lens examine the claims

A magnifying glass with an open book inside it — Acts 17:11, searching the Scriptures to see whether these things are so. This is the most accurate description of what the site actually does: it examines claims against the text. The gold handle gives it a strong diagonal, which makes it the most recognisable of the six at small sizes.

03The Question the name, drawn

The question mark's stem descends straight into the book's gutter — the book is the mark's full stop. It's the wordmark compressed into a symbol, so it reinforces "Which Gospel?" every time it appears. The simplest silhouette here, and the one that would survive being stamped on a watch face or a business card.

My read

02 (Berean Lens) and 03 (The Question) are the two I'd take further. 02 describes the method and has the strongest small-size silhouette; 03 is the site's name drawn as a picture, which is the cheapest kind of brand recall you can get.

01 is the best "explains itself in one second to a stranger" option if you want to lean on the fork metaphor. 04 is the safe choice if you'd rather evolve the ⚖ you already have than replace it.
