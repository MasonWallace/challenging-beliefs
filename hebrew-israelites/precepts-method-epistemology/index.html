Isaiah 28:10 is mockery, not instruction

Unanswered

The camps never answer this objection. Ask it, then wait.

How the camps read

By chaining verses. Lines from all over the Bible are strung together by shared words, regardless of what surrounds them.

The method is justified from Isaiah 28:10 — "precept must be upon precept… line upon line… here a little, and there a little."

Why the method matters more than any verse

Because it can produce conclusions no chapter states on its own. And it is close to unanswerable if you argue verse by verse, which is why the response is to read whole chapters instead.

Read three verses further

Isaiah 28:10 is not instruction. It is mockery — drunken priests imitating the prophet's speech as baby-talk.

Then verse 13 turns their own words into the judgment. The word of the LORD becomes to them precept upon precept.

They go, and fall backward, and are broken.

Their answer, at full strength

That Scripture interprets Scripture, and the objection is a guild protecting itself. Paul compares spiritual things with spiritual.

The Bereans searched daily. Jesus expounded himself from Moses and all the prophets.

Concede the class argument, and mean it — gatekeeping who may read the Bible is a real sin with a long history.

How strong is this argument?

Strong for you: camp materials do not address what Isaiah 28:13 does with the phrase.

What to say

"Read me Isaiah 28 verses 9 through 13. Who is talking in verse 10?"

How they answer this — at its strongest

Scripture interprets Scripture. Demanding Greek is how the text was kept away.

Their defence is that Scripture interprets Scripture. The objection, they say, is a guild protecting itself. Comparing spiritual things with spiritual is Paul's own instruction (1 Corinthians 2:13). The Bereans searched the scriptures daily (Acts 17:11). Jesus expounded Himself from Moses and all the prophets, across the whole canon (Luke 24:27). And prophecy is not of any private interpretation (2 Peter 1:20).

Before a man may read his own Bible, he is asked for seminary training, Greek and Hebrew, and approved commentaries. That is exactly the gatekeeping that kept enslaved people from the text in the first place. It is being used again, they argue, the moment ordinary people reach conclusions the institution dislikes.

They also note, with some justice, that their opponents chain verses too. They just call it systematic theology.

The verdict

Strong for you. Camp materials never touch what Isaiah 28:13 does with the phrase.

Graded unrefuted, because the objection is not addressed in camp materials at all. That objection is simple. Isaiah 28:10 is a mocker's taunt, turned into judgment in verse 13.

Concede the class argument first, and mean it. Keeping Scripture from ordinary readers is a real historical sin. No one needs Greek to be saved. But the translation debate over tsav latsav qav laqav is a mainstream scholarly one, not an elite invention. Some readings do take verse 10 as plain teaching. Even those make it a picture of patient teaching, not a warrant for cutting the text into pieces.

The internal clashes are the sharpest point. A movement that stakes everything on Hebrew identity binds itself to a seventeenth-century English Bible. It invents sounds with no manuscript behind them. And it quotes a book it does not accept as canon.
