The SPLC lists three camps as extremist

Contested

Both sides hold something true here. Say so out loud.

What the listing is

The Southern Poverty Law Center keeps a file on Radical Hebrew Israelites. It names Great Millstone, ISUPK and IUIC as the biggest listed groups.

Read their own notes on it: they no longer list the movement as a whole.

Where the camps are right

A listing is not an answer to a doctrine. Most members are not violent.

The SPLC has its own money to raise and a record of listings people fought over. And using the label on Black religious groups, while calling much the same talk elsewhere ordinary politics, is a fair thing to object to.

Where the critics are right

The teaching that is listed is real, published, and on video. Nobody had to invent it.

Some individuals citing the identity have committed violence, and the movement's own moderates say so.

How strong is this argument?

Genuinely arguable, both ways — on purpose. Both sides hold something true here, and this site should show that rather than pick a team.

Why this helps you

Knowing the difference between the listing and the doctrine means you can talk about the doctrine without handing anyone an easy escape.

What to say

"I'm not interested in the hate-group label. I'm interested in what this specific teaching says."

How they answer this — at its strongest

A listing is not an answer. Most members are peaceful street preachers.

The camps reject the listing as a political weapon. SPLC has its own funding incentives, they argue, and a record of listings people fought over. Using the hate-group label on Black religious movements is one thing. Treating much the same talk elsewhere as ordinary politics is another. That is exactly the double standard they preach about.

They note that their members are overwhelmingly non-violent. They note that the camps run drug-recovery, marriage and money-discipline programmes where nobody else does. And street preaching that offends is still speech.

On Jersey City they point out, correctly, that no group link was ever shown. Being loud and being disliked is not the same as being a threat. The label, they argue, keeps people from ever hearing what is actually said.

The verdict

Both sides hold something true. Argue the claims, never the label.

Graded contested on purpose. Both sides hold something true, and the site should show that.

The camps are right on four points. A listing is not a refutation. Most members are not violent. The Jersey City attackers were never shown to belong to any group. And the community work is real. Critics are right that the talk the ADL and SPLC quote is real, and taken from public teaching, and that it has consequences.

The method matters more than the verdict here. A hate-group listing cannot settle whether Deuteronomy 28 predicts the Middle Passage. Citing it as though it could is a way of avoiding the argument. Equally, calling SPLC biased does not unsay what was said on camera. Argue the claims. Cite incidents precisely. Never let a label do the work of an answer.
