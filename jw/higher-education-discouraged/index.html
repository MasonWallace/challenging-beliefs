Which Gospel? › Jehovah's Witnesses

9% hold degrees, the lowest of any US religion

Admitted

The Watchtower's own publications admit these facts. You can make this point from their library alone.

What has been taught

For decades, university has been described as a spiritual danger. The Watchtower of October 1, 2005 (pp.

26-31) urged young ones toward theocratic goals instead. Governing Body member Anthony Morris III gave a broadcast in May 2017 called "What Is the Best Education?", urging Bethel service over degrees.

In 2023 Stephen Lett compared sending a child to university to a school of hungry sharks. Circuit overseers have been directed that an elder whose family pursue higher education may lose his appointment.

What it has cost, in numbers

Pew Research measured it. About 9% of US Jehovah's Witnesses hold a bachelor's degree.

That is the lowest of any major US religious group surveyed, against a national average of about 35%. Household incomes track it.

The exception at headquarters

Their own senior counsel, Philip Brumley, holds a law degree. Bethel keeps qualified lawyers, doctors and engineers on hand.

Those are exactly the skills the counsel would have kept members from getting.

Their answer, at full strength

They have never banned education. They require literacy and teach millions to read, and they leave schooling to families.

Their counsel weighs real data that secular writers also raise: student debt, campus culture, graduates in jobs that do not need degrees. Vocational training has been encouraged for years.

And the priority is spiritual. If the Kingdom is real, seeking it first (Matthew 6:33) is rational, and warning against careerism is what any serious faith does.

How strong is this argument?

They admit this themselves. The counsel is in their own broadcasts and magazines, and the outcome is measured by neutral survey data.

A 2025 Governing Body update reframed education as a personal choice. That is genuine, and it arrived after the generations who obeyed had paid for it.

Scripture never treats learning as apostasy. Moses was schooled in all Egypt's wisdom (Acts 7:22).

Paul studied under Gamaliel (Acts 22:3). "The plans of the diligent one surely make for advantage" (Proverbs 21:5).

What to say

"Moses was trained in all the wisdom of Egypt and Paul studied under Gamaliel. What changed?"

How they answer this — at its strongest

Education was never banned. If the Kingdom is real, seeking it first makes sense.

The Witness defense is that education was never banned. They require literacy and teach millions to read, and they leave schooling to families.

Their counsel weighs real data that secular writers raise too. Student debt. Campus culture. Graduates in jobs that do not need a degree. Trade training has been urged for years.

And the claim is about priority. If the Kingdom is real, seeking it first (Matthew 6:33) is rational. Warning against careerism is what any serious faith does.

The verdict

They admit it themselves. Pew measured the cost: 9% hold a degree.

The counsel is in their own broadcasts and magazines. The outcome is measured by neutral survey data from Pew. So both ends of the chain are documented.

The 'personal choice' framing is genuine, but it arrived only in 2025. And the effect on elder appointments is documented in circuit direction.

Severity medium. The costs are real and countable, though they are not bodily harm.
