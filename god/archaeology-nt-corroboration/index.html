Pilate's name in stone, Caiaphas's bones in a box

Conceded

Skeptical scholars grant this themselves. Argue it from their sources, not yours.

Names in stone

In 1961 a block of stone at Caesarea Maritima turned up with Pontius Pilate's name on it. It gives his title as prefect of Judea.

That is the right one for his years. Tacitus got it wrong and wrote procurator.

In 1990 a carved bone-box marked "Joseph son of Caiaphas" was found in Jerusalem. Very likely the high priest of the passion story.

John's pools

John's Gospel was long accused of inventing its geography. It describes a Pool of Bethesda with five colonnades, which is an odd number for a building.

Excavation found a double pool. Four porticoes around it, and one across the middle.

Five. The Pool of Siloam of John 9 was uncovered in 2004, during a sewer repair.

Nazareth

René Salm and other online mythicists claim Nazareth did not exist in the first century. It is settled.

A house from that century was dug up and published in 2009 by the Israel Antiquities Authority. There are the Nazareth Village Farm remains.

And a stone list from Caesarea names the town among the priestly courses. The claim is so weak that Tim O'Neill, an atheist, debunks it to keep his own side honest.

Their answer, at full strength

Accurate scenery is not accurate miracles. Gone with the Wind gets Atlanta right.

The serious version runs like this. John's local knowledge shows his tradition carries real memory of Jerusalem before AD 70.

Critical scholars grant that and still date the Gospel to about 90-110, with no witness as author. Local colour survives in communities, not only in witnesses.

The Caiaphas identification is debated, since the spelling and the tomb's plainness give some scholars pause. And Pilate and Caiaphas were famous.

A story set in Judea could hardly miss them.
