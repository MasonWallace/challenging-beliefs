Most scholars date Daniel to the 160s BC

Contested

Serious philosophers argue this both ways. Say so before your friend does.

Say the consensus first

Most critical scholars date the final form of Daniel to the 160s BC. The case for that is serious.

State it before anyone else does.

Why they date it late

Daniel 11 tracks the wars of the Greek successor kingdoms with uncanny precision, right up to Antiochus IV Epiphanes. Then verses 40-45 predict his end, and it does not match how he actually died in 163 BC.

The pagan critic Porphyry noticed that first. It is the classic signature of prophecy written during the events, failing only where it goes past its author's own day.

Add Greek loanwords, Daniel's place outside the Prophets in the Hebrew canon, and Sirach's silence about him around 180 BC.

The minority answer

K. A.

Kitchen argues that Daniel's Aramaic fits an earlier Imperial profile. Daniel also knows that Belshazzar ruled as coregent while Nabonidus was away.

Later Greek historians lost that detail, and it was recovered only from cuneiform. That is hard for a writer in the 160s to know.

Qumran holds several Daniel manuscripts from about 125 BC. Sixty years from writing to copies, plus fast standing as scripture, is tight for a fresh forgery.

Their answer, at full strength

John J. Collins wrote the standard critical commentary.

Chapter 11's accuracy, then failure at verse 40, is as close to a dating fingerprint as ancient writing offers. The genre, apocalyptic work under a borrowed name, was common, and the first readers likely knew the convention.

The Belshazzar detail could survive in Babylonian-Jewish memory. The Prayer of Nabonidus at Qumran shows such traditions circulating.

Few Aramaic specialists find the language argument decisive. And 1 Enoch won acceptance fast too.

On headcount, this is not close.

How strong is this argument?

Contested, and be candid. The early date is the minority position.

So do not lead with Daniel against an informed skeptic. You will spend the whole evening on Aramaic.

The durable point is the fallback. Even at 165 BC, Daniel 7's "son of man" and Daniel 9's timetable are older than Jesus.

They shaped the hope he walked into.

What to say
