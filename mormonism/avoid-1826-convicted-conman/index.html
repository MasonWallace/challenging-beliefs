Joseph Smith was not convicted in 1826

Don't use

The LDS answer holds up. Do not lead with this one.

The claim to stop making

That Joseph Smith was a convicted con man. That a court convicted him of fraud in 1826, just before he found the plates.

What actually happened

The 1826 Bainbridge proceeding was real. Smith was 20.

He was charged as a "disorderly person" over paid glass looking. Justice Neely's own fee bill calls him "The Glass Looker."

Why the word convicted fails

It was probably a preliminary examination, not a trial. The surviving records are fragmentary and they contradict each other.

The Pearsall account says guilty. The fee bill and mittimus evidence point the other way.

Scholars split — Gordon Madsen reads it as a discharge, while Dan Vogel and Wesley Walters disagree. Convicted states as fact what the documents cannot carry.

What survives without a verdict

The occupation. By the church's own Gospel Topics essay, and by Richard Bushman in Rough Stone Rolling, Smith was a paid treasure-seer.

He hunted buried valuables by looking at a stone in his hat. It was the same stone, in the same hat, that produced the Book of Mormon.

How strong is this argument?

Do not lead with it, and never say convicted. Lead with the seer-stone facts the church admits, which are in the translation case.

Then let the 1826 record confirm the timeline.

What to say

"The 1826 papers call him 'The Glass Looker.' Same stone, same hat, a few years later."

How they answer this — at its strongest

It was likely a first hearing, not a trial, and the records do not agree.

That is overclaimed.

The 1826 Bainbridge proceeding was real. Smith, aged 20, was charged as a 'disorderly person' over paid 'glass looking'. Justice Neely's own fee bill labels him 'The Glass Looker'.

But it was likely a preliminary examination. The surviving records are fragmentary and they contradict each other. The Pearsall account says 'guilty', while the fee bill and mittimus evidence point the other way.

Scholars dispute the outcome. Gordon Madsen reads an acquittal or discharge. Dan Vogel and Wesley Walters disagree. So 'convicted of fraud' states as fact what the documents cannot establish.

The verdict

Never say convicted: lead with the seer stone the church itself admits.

Say 'convicted' and the apologist wins the exchange on the record's ambiguity. And that ambiguity is genuine, not spin.

Here is the core that needs no conviction. By the church's own Gospel Topics admission, and by Richard Bushman in Rough Stone Rolling, Smith was a paid treasure-seer. He hunted buried valuables by staring at a stone in his hat. It was the same stone, in the same hat, that he then used to produce the Book of Mormon.

The 1826 court documents back up that occupation whatever the verdict. Lead with the church-admitted seer-stone facts, and let the 1826 record confirm the timeline.
