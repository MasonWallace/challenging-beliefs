Every human a spirit child of Heavenly Parents

Unrefuted

No good counter-argument has been published by LDS scholars.

What is taught

That every human lived before birth as a literal spirit child of Heavenly Parents. The essay Mother in Heaven says all human beings "are beloved spirit children of heavenly parents." A First Presidency statement of 1909, quoted in Gospel Principles chapter 2, says man as a spirit "was begotten and born of heavenly parents." Abraham 3:22-23 describes intelligences organised before the world.

See also D&C 93:29 and Moses 3:5.

What the Bible says

1 Corinthians 15:46 puts the order the other way: "that was not first which is spiritual, but that which is natural." Zechariah 12:1 says the LORD "formeth the spirit of man within him." And in Genesis 2:7 man becomes a living soul when God breathes into formed dust.

Their answer, at full strength

FAIR builds a real biblical case. God knew Jeremiah "before I formed thee in the belly" at Jeremiah 1:5.

The sons of God "shouted for joy" when the earth was founded, in Job 38:4-7. At death the spirit "shall return unto God who gave it," in Ecclesiastes 12:7 — and return implies having been there.

Hebrews 12:9 calls God "the Father of spirits." The disciples' question in John 9:2 seems to allow for it. Origen held that souls existed before bodies, before the church condemned it in the sixth century.

And on 1 Corinthians 15:46 they say Paul is talking about resurrection bodies.

How strong is this argument?

Strong for you, and give them one point. FAIR is right that 1 Corinthians 15:46 is about resurrection bodies, so do not lean on it.

The rest dissolves in context. Jeremiah 1:5 is about God choosing, as in Romans 8:29.

The sons of God in Job 38:7 are angels, and God's whole point is that Job was not there. Ecclesiastes 12:7 pairs with dust going back to earth.

And Origen taught nothing like heavenly parents giving birth to spirits. That specific claim has no biblical support at all.

What to say

"Which verse says our spirits were born to heavenly parents?"

How they answer this — at its strongest

Jeremiah 1:5, Job 38:7 and Hebrews 12:9 point to a life before birth.

FAIR assembles a biblical case for life before birth. God knew Jeremiah 'before I formed thee in the belly', at Jeremiah 1:5. The 'sons of God shouted for joy' when the earth was founded, at Job 38:4-7.

At death 'the spirit shall return unto God who gave it', at Ecclesiastes 12:7. To return is to have been there. Hebrews 12:9 calls God 'the Father of spirits'. And the disciples' question about the man born blind, at John 9:2, seems to allow for choices made before birth.

FAIR adds that souls existing before bodies was a live view in early Christianity. Origen held it, and it was condemned only in the sixth century. On 1 Corinthians 15:46 they answer that Paul's subject is resurrection bodies, not where spirits come from.

The verdict

Strong for you, but grant them 1 Corinthians 15:46, which is about the risen body.

The teaching is documented from current official sources, and the core conflict stands. No biblical text says human spirits were begotten by Heavenly Parents before the world. Zechariah 12:1 puts the forming of a man's spirit 'within him'.

The proof-texts thin out in context. Jeremiah 1:5 is the language of choosing and foreknowing, as at Romans 8:29. The 'sons of God' at Job 38:7 are angels, and God's point to Job is that Job was not there. 'Return' at Ecclesiastes 12:7 pairs with dust going back to earth.

FAIR is right about 1 Corinthians 15:46, so do not lean on it. And Origen shows the idea was thinkable early, though his version had no Heavenly Mother and no spirit birth.

The distinctive claim, that human spirits were literally born to heavenly parents, has no biblical support at all.
