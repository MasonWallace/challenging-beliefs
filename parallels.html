The same pattern, again and again

Four movements, born centuries and continents apart, that have never coordinated with each other — and that keep arriving at the same handful of structures. This page sets those structures side by side, and then applies one biblical test evenly to all of them, including to us.

Why this page exists

Most people encounter these movements one at a time, and each one is argued about on its own terms: was Jerusalem destroyed in 607 or 587, is almah a virgin, what does machmad mean. Those arguments matter, and the rest of this site is full of them.

But something else is visible only when you stand back. A movement that claims a new revelation which corrects the Bible tends to need the same set of parts to work — a private channel of revelation, a reason the existing Scriptures cannot be trusted, and a cost for leaving. Those parts show up independently, again and again, in groups that would each be horrified to be compared with the others.

That recurrence is the argument. Not "look how strange they are" — a Muslim can point at the Book of Mormon and a Latter-day Saint can point at the Quran, and both would be doing the same thing. The point is that Scripture supplies a test that does not care which movement is in front of you, and that applies to us in exactly the same way.

The recurring structures

1 · One man, one angel, no witnesses

A new revelation that corrects the existing Scriptures arrives privately, to a single individual, with no second witness to the event itself.

Mormonism

Moroni appears to Joseph Smith alone. The earliest published accounts name the angel Nephi, later changed to Moroni; the First Vision itself has four differing firsthand accounts.

Islam

Gabriel appears to Muhammad alone in the cave at Hira. The earliest account has him terrified and doubting the source, reassured by his wife and her Christian cousin.

Jehovah's Witnesses

No angel. Russell's authority came from chronology, not visitation — and the organization later located its own appointment in an invisible 1919 inspection that likewise has no witnesses.

Hebrew Israelites

No founding revelation claim — the movement rests on a reading of Deuteronomy 28, not on a visitation.

2 · The Bible had to be broken first

Before a new authority can be accepted, the existing Scriptures must be shown to be corrupted, mistranslated, or insufficient. In every case the correction arrives from the same source as the new revelation.

Mormonism

"Many plain and precious things" removed — and Joseph's corrected Bible, which his own church never adopted.

Islam

Tahrif — but corruption of the text is a late doctrine; early Islam taught corruption of meaning, and the Quran itself endorses the Scripture of its own day.

Jehovah's Witnesses

An anonymous committee produces the organization's own Bible — the only translation used in its meetings and literature.

Hebrew Israelites

The King James text is kept, but read through a method — chaining single lines "precept upon precept" — that lets any chapter mean what the chain requires.

3 · An authority that cannot be checked

The channel is placed beyond correction — either because it is inspired, or, more ingeniously, because it is not inspired and therefore cannot be held to a prophet's standard while still being obeyed like one.

Mormonism

The prophet "will never lead the church astray" — while past teachings that failed are reclassified as personal opinion.

Islam

The seal of the prophets, with abrogation built in: a later revelation can cancel an earlier one, and the cancelling verse comes from the same channel.

Jehovah's Witnesses

The sharpest version anywhere: the Governing Body says it is neither inspired nor infallible — and disagreement is still grounds for losing your family.

Hebrew Israelites

Camp elders and the teaching lineage, with no external check — and the movement has splintered repeatedly over exactly that.

4 · Dated predictions, and what happens when they fail

The one structure Scripture explicitly tells us to test (Deuteronomy 18:22). Notice that the failure is rarely admitted — it is re-dated, spiritualized, or reassigned.

Mormonism

The Independence temple "in this generation," the Kirtland bank, and the rest — measured against Deuteronomy 18.

Islam

Not this pattern. The relevant question is the opposite one — the absence of predictive prophecy offered as a sign.

Jehovah's Witnesses

The most documented case of all: 1914, 1925, 1975, and a "generation" redefined four times.

Hebrew Israelites

A dated year-2000 prediction, its failure, a rebrand, and a succession — all in the movement's own record.

5 · A price for leaving

The load-bearing structure. Where the exit is expensive, no other question ever gets examined honestly — which is why this, not the doctrine, is usually what actually holds a person in place.

Mormonism

Formally discouraged, practically real: family strain, temple exclusion, and a community that was the whole of your social world.

Islam

Classical jurisprudence prescribes death for apostasy; in most of the world today the cost is family, honor, and community rather than law.

Jehovah's Witnesses

The most systematized version in existence: organized shunning including family, with resignation treated exactly like expulsion since 1981.

Hebrew Israelites

Camp expulsion and the loss of a recovered identity — leaving means going back to being "asleep," which is designed to be unthinkable.

6 · The two-tier believer

A category of insider who has access the ordinary member does not — and an ordinary member whose standing is permanently provisional.

Mormonism

Temple recommend holders and the endowed, versus members who may not enter the building their tithing built.

Islam

Not structurally — though scholarly gatekeeping over who may interpret functions similarly in practice.

Jehovah's Witnesses

The starkest example anywhere: 144,000 anointed and a great crowd, where 99.9% of members decline the bread and cup at their one annual observance.

Hebrew Israelites

The tribes and the heathen — in the strongest camp teaching, salvation is bounded by descent.

The test that applies to all of them — and to us

Galatians 1:8

"But though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you than that which we have preached unto you, let him be accursed."Paul, to the churches of Galatia — decades before any of the movements on this page existed

Read what that verse actually does. It does not say an angel could not appear. It grants the possibility and then removes its authority: the message is tested by the gospel already delivered, not the other way round. And Paul includes himself and his own team in the sentence — though we — which is what makes it a test rather than a weapon.

That is why this is the right frame for the parallel, and "look how strange the other one is" is not. If the Gabriel account cannot establish a gospel that corrects Paul's, neither can the Moroni account, and neither can anything an angel says to me tomorrow. One rule, applied evenly, with no exemption written in for the person holding it.

Two further tests do the same work: Deuteronomy 18:22 — the prophet whose word does not come to pass has not spoken from the LORD, which is a public, checkable standard and not a matter of interpretation; and 2 Corinthians 11:14 — Satan himself is transformed into an angel of light, which is precisely why a luminous messenger is not self-authenticating.

How to use this — and how not to

Apply the test to yourself first, out loud

"If an angel appeared to me tomorrow with a new gospel, I'd have to reject it too — Paul says so, and he includes himself." Say that before you apply it to anyone. It converts an accusation into a shared standard, and it is the only version of this argument that is honest.

Use it to explain, not to corner

The most useful thing this page can do in a conversation is answer the question "why don't you accept our founder's revelation?" The answer is not "because he's yours" — it is a rule that existed centuries before him and that rules out a great many claims your friend also rejects.

Be honest about where the parallel breaks

Several cells on this page say "not this pattern," and they say it because it is true. A parallel that has to be forced is worth less than none, and the moment you stretch one, everything else you have said becomes suspect.

Don't argue "your religion is as crazy as theirs"

This is the trap. It is a tu quoque — an argument from mutual embarrassment — and it proves nothing about whether either claim is true. It also insults two people at once, and your friend will rightly notice that the same move works against you.

Don't bring another religion into the room uninvited

Telling a Latter-day Saint about Muhammad usually produces a defense of Joseph Smith rather than an examination of him. Pattern-matching works best after someone has already begun to ask questions — as a frame for what they are seeing, not as an opening move.

Don't use the pattern as a substitute for the evidence

Structural similarity is suggestive, not decisive. Two things sharing a shape can still differ in truth. The parallels are a lens for reading the cases; they are not a replacement for them, and a conversation that never gets to a specific document has not really happened.

The honest version of the argument

Stated fairly, it comes to this. Christianity makes a claim of exactly the kind that ought to be tested this way, and it invites the test: a public execution under a named Roman official, and a resurrection proclaimed within weeks in the very city where the body could have been produced, to hundreds of people who were still alive and could be asked. Paul stakes the entire religion on it and says plainly that if it did not happen, the whole thing is worthless.

That is a different shape of claim from a private revelation to one man that cannot, even in principle, be checked by anyone else. It is not that Christians are exempt from the test — it is that the test is the reason to look at the evidence, and the evidence is public. The Case for God lays that out from sources that do not want the conclusion.
